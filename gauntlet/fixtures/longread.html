Kestrel Ridge: the long report

A complete operational history of the site, section by section.

Section 1

The power supply was islanded for the first six years, running on a diesel plant that was replaced only after a prolonged outage.

Data was written to tape until surprisingly late, and the migration of that archive took two people the better part of a year.

Visiting observers were housed in a converted farm building, which flooded twice.

The weather station on the ridge has recorded continuously since installation and its record is now used well beyond astronomy.

The valley floor holds mist until nearly ten in the morning through most of the autumn, which the survey team had not accounted for in the original schedule.

Procurement ran to eleven separate contracts, of which four were terminated early and two were renegotiated after the first winter.

Section 2

Staff turnover in the second and third years was high enough that the site log has gaps, and these gaps are noted here rather than quietly filled in.

A second access road was proposed, costed, and abandoned; the single-track approach remains the principal logistical constraint for the site.

Instrument calibration drifted measurably with temperature, and the correction curve derived in the fourth year is still in use today.

Local opposition was limited but persistent, focused mainly on night-time vehicle movements along the ridge road.
